# Coordinate Normalization Strategy: SignTalk AI

**Document ID:** STAI-P2P2-002  
**Project:** SignTalk AI  
**Phase:** Phase 2 — Part 2  
**Status:** Approved  

---

## 1. Motivation & Mathematical Objective

Raw coordinates generated by MediaPipe reside in normalized camera screen space $[0.0, 1.0] \times [0.0, 1.0] \times \mathbb{R}$. Directly feeding raw coordinates into spatial graph convolutions causes models to overfit to:
1. **Camera Distance / Framing:** A signer sitting 0.6m from the webcam appears much larger than a signer 1.8m away.
2. **Signer Physical Stature:** Arm span and shoulder width variations between different users.
3. **Lateral Offsets:** Signer sitting slightly left or right of the optical center.

The mathematical goal is an affine isometric transformation that eliminates translation and scale dependence while strictly preserving **intra-joint angles, bone lengths ratios, and spatial movement velocities**.

---

## 2. Torso Centering and Scale Normalization Mathematics

Let $\mathbf{p}_{t, i} = [x_{t, i}, y_{t, i}, z_{t, i}]^T \in \mathbb{R}^3$ denote the raw 3D position of joint $i \in \{0, \dots, 92\}$ at frame $t$.

### 2.1 Anatomical Reference Points
The upper-body pose provides two robust anchors:
- $\mathbf{p}_{t, 47}$: Left Shoulder coordinate.
- $\mathbf{p}_{t, 48}$: Right Shoulder coordinate.

### 2.2 Instantaneous Mid-Shoulder Center & Scale
For any frame $t$ with valid shoulder detections:
$$\mathbf{c}_{t, torso} = \frac{\mathbf{p}_{t, 47} + \mathbf{p}_{t, 48}}{2}$$

The instantaneous scale factor corresponds to the Euclidean bi-acromial diameter (shoulder width):
$$s_{t, torso} = \|\mathbf{p}_{t, 47} - \mathbf{p}_{t, 48}\|_2$$

To prevent singularity or division-by-zero during rare lateral occlusions:
$$s_t = \max(s_{t, torso}, \epsilon), \quad \epsilon = 1.0 \times 10^{-4}$$

### 2.3 Sequence-Smoothed Global Reference
To prevent "breathing" artifacts (where slight shoulder movements during breathing artificially compress or dilate the hand trajectory), SignTalk AI precomputes a sequence-smoothed global anchor across all valid frames $\mathcal{T}_{valid} = \{t \mid \text{mask}[t, 47] \land \text{mask}[t, 48]\}$:

$$\bar{\mathbf{c}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{valid}|} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_{valid}} \mathbf{c}_{t, torso}$$
$$\bar{s} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{valid}|} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_{valid}} s_{t, torso}$$

### 2.4 Final Normalization Transformation
$$\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{t, i} = \frac{\mathbf{p}_{t, i} - \bar{\mathbf{c}}}{\bar{s}}, \quad \forall i \in \{0, \dots, 92\}$$

For the depth dimension $z$, an optional depth scaling factor $\gamma_z = 1.0$ is maintained:
$$\hat{z}_{t, i} = \frac{z_{t, i} - \bar{c}_z}{\bar{s}} \cdot \gamma_z$$

## 3. Geometric Invariance Properties

1. **Mid-Shoulder Fixed Origin:** The average mid-shoulder point maps exactly to $[0.0, 0.0, 0.0]$.
2. **Standardized Shoulder Span:** The left shoulder is positioned at roughly $[-0.5, 0.0, 0.0]$ and the right shoulder at roughly $[+0.5, 0.0, 0.0]$.
3. **Scale Invariance:** All coordinates represent units of "shoulder widths", rendering the model completely invariant to whether the user sits 0.5m or 2.0m from the lens.
4. **Preservation of Hand Relative Trajectory:** Crucially, hand positions are normalized relative to the body torso rather than isolated bounding boxes, preserving the vital spatial relationship between hands, face, and chest (e.g. distinguishing a hand touching the chin vs. hovering in front of the chest).
